# Originality: how to not make the same video as everyone else

Read this before every concept. It overrides every example, demo and recipe in this skill.

## The problem
Given the same brief, every model (and most people) lands on the same first idea, because it's the most probable one. For any AI or software product that idea is:

> a pain line → typing a request in a close-up → the result builds itself → edit it by asking → "Published / Exported" toasts → a montage of other uses → "Describe it. Get the X." → logo.

A blind test proved it: four separate runs on one brief, with and without this skill, on three different models, all made that film. **That film is now banned as a starting point.** If your concept can be summarised as "type → result → montage → slogan", throw it away and start again.

The examples in this skill (the demos, the field guide, the style recipes, the films we made) show *mechanics*: how to move things. They are not structures. Never reproduce their order of shots.

## The method (do it every time, even working alone)

### 1. Find the human truth before the product
Answer in plain words, without mentioning the product:
- Who exactly is this for, in one specific person? ("a café owner doing customer feedback on a Sunday night", not "users")
- What does their life look like *before*? What do they physically do, see, touch, lose, wait for?
- What is the feeling the product removes or gives? (dread, boredom, shame, delight, relief, pride)
- What is the one moment of change, in their world, not in the UI?

### 2. Write 8 concepts from 8 different angles
One line each, **each from a different angle**, no two with the same shot structure:
1. **A moment in a life:** one specific person, one specific day or hour.
2. **The physical world:** the subject as objects (forms are paper, clipboards, queues, a visa application, a wedding RSVP in the post, sticky notes on a fridge).
3. **The before is the hero:** spend most of the film on the absurd, recognisable pain, then end in two seconds of relief.
4. **A character:** an object or a mascot with a personality and a voice.
5. **Pure type:** no UI at all; the words *are* the visuals.
6. **Data or proof:** one number or result made unforgettable.
7. **A form borrowed from elsewhere:** a recipe card, a nature documentary, a heist, a cooking show, an instruction manual, a sports replay, a weather forecast, a dating profile, a museum label, a receipt.
8. **A single continuous shot:** one camera move through one world, no cuts.

### 3. Kill tests (cross out any that fail)
- **The swap test:** replace the product name with a competitor's. If the concept still works, it's generic. Cross it out.
- **The cliché test:** does it use anything from the banned list below as its *backbone*? Cross it out.
- **The "seen it" test:** could you name three launch videos that already do this? Cross it out.
- **The sell test:** after watching, would the viewer know what the product does and want it? If not, cross it out.

### 4. Choose and justify
Pick the most surprising survivor that still sells. Write down the three most obvious ideas you rejected, and why. When a person is available, show them the two best survivors as stills. When working alone, take the boldest survivor, not the safest.

### 5. Let the concept choose the world, the style and the moves
The concept decides what the frame is made of: paper, a kitchen, a stage, a map, a single word, a character's face. Only then pick a style (styles.md) and moves (moves.md) that serve it. The UI may be a small part of the film, or absent until the last seconds. A product film does not have to be made of product screens.

## Banned as a backbone (allowed only as a small, fresh beat, or when the person asks)
- Typing a prompt in a close-up as the opening or the main beat.
- "Drag. Drop. Repeat. Or just say it." and every "the old way, crossed out" opener.
- "Describe it. Get the ___." / "Just say it." / "It's that simple."
- The result building itself section by section as the climax.
- "Publish / Export / Sent ✓" toasts as the payoff.
- A fast montage of other use-cases near the end.
- A wall of cards diving into one.
- A slogan, then the logo, then the URL, all centred on white.
- A headline over every scene.

## Originality checklist (add to the quality score)
- Could only this product have this film? (swap test)
- Does it look and *feel* different from the last film you made with this skill?
- Is there at least one image a viewer will remember tomorrow that isn't a screenshot?
- Did you write down the obvious ideas you rejected?
