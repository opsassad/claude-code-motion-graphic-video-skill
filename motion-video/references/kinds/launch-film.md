# Launch film

**What it is:** the premium reveal of a product or a major feature. People should finish it knowing what it is, why it matters, and wanting it.
**Pick it when:** something new ships and it deserves a moment: on the website, LinkedIn, X, a launch post, an event screen.
**Length:** 20–60 s. 16:9 for web and events, 1:1 or 4:5 for feeds.

## Ask the person
- What is new, in one sentence? What can people do now that they couldn't before?
- Which 1–3 capabilities must appear? Which is the "aha"?
- Who is it for (one specific person)?
- Is there a proof point (a result, a number, a customer quote) we can show truthfully?

## Shapes it can take (choose from the concept, or invent one)
- **The aha first:** open on the single most surprising capability, then pull back to what the product is.
- **One object's journey:** follow one real thing (a document, an order, a message) through the product to its outcome.
- **Before is the hero:** spend most of the film on the recognisable pain, then a short, satisfying relief.
- **Scale reveal:** one small, intimate moment that turns out to be one of thousands.

## Worlds and looks that suit
Clean light product film, cinematic product film, atmospheric glass (styles.md). The product's own brand world first.

## Moves that often fit
A hero object with `glowRim`; product states (`thinking`, `streamText`, `scanReveal`, `flip`); camera on real UI with `cursor` + `clickZoom`; `wall` for one-to-many; `wordToSentence` for the line that names the product.

## Seen in the study
- A version reveal: logo → a personal greeting → drag-and-drop with weight → the result scanning in → pulling back into a laptop in a real set.
- An "Apple-style" chat launch: angled long lists that resolve flat, one word growing into a sentence, claims as small infographics.

## Pitfalls
The banned default backbone (originality.md). A list of features with equal weight. Headlines that repeat what the picture already says.
