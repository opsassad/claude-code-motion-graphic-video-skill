# Explainer

**What it is:** shows how something works: a workflow, an automation, a flow of data, what happens behind the button. Clarity first, but still beautiful.
**Pick it when:** the product is new to people, or its value is in a process they can't see.
**Length:** 30–90 s. 16:9 for web and decks, 1:1 for feeds.

## Ask the person
- What is the process, step by step, in their words?
- What goes in and what comes out? Where does the magic happen?
- Who must understand it (customers, buyers, your own team)? Do they need voice-over?
- Any steps that are commonly misunderstood?

## Shapes it can take
- **Follow one item through the machine:** one email, one order, one link click, from arrival to outcome.
- **Assemble the system:** pieces fly in and lock into place, then the system runs once end to end.
- **Problem → mechanism → result:** the pain in one beat, the how in three, the outcome as proof.
- **Zoom levels:** start at the outcome, zoom into how each part produces it.

## Worlds and looks
Clean light product film (white, a faint grid, one accent) or a narrated explainer. Diagrams are fine here: nodes, lines, ribbons.

## Moves that often fit
`drawPath` between nodes with dots travelling; steps flying in tilted and assembling into a straight card; a lit path through a grid (`wall` + highlights); `wordTexture` (one action among many); `cascade` for results arriving; text and UI inline in sentences; `countUp` for outcomes.

## Seen in the study
- Workflow automation: steps fly in and assemble into the workflow card; a lit path through a grid of starter chips; an hourglass of blue liquid for "time saved"; emails getting a "To respond" label.
- Analytics: a flowing ribbon (one colour per funnel stage) with stat cards at each stage; a filter asked in plain English that redraws the chart; a node diagram for webhooks.
- AI memory: data lines converging into the product icon; a ticker of problem questions; a counter in a pill with halo rings.

## Pitfalls
Too many steps (cut to the 3 that matter). Diagram soup. Explaining what the viewer can already see.
