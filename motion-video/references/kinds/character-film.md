# Character film

**What it is:** a character (a mascot, an illustrated agent, a personified object) carries the film with a personality and a voice.
**Pick it when:** the brand has an illustration system or mascot, or the product is an agent/assistant/bot people should feel something for.
**Length:** 30–90 s.

## Ask the person
- Is there an existing character or illustration style? (files, brand guide)
- What's its personality in three words? How does it talk?
- What does it do in the product (assist, build, check, remind)?

## Shapes it can take
- **Meet the character:** it's created and personalised, then does its job, then shows it's one of a team.
- **A day on the job:** the character tackles three tasks, reacting to each.
- **The character vs the chaos:** the mess grows; the character calmly sorts it.

## Worlds and looks
The brand's illustration world on its neutral ground; real UI floating on a solid brand colour. Character-led (styles.md).

## Moves that often fit
A personalisation carousel (one item centred in a selection box); `thinking` with the character's face; `cascade` for work getting done; source counts with `countUp`; `wall` pull-back from one character to a whole team; copy written in the character's voice.

## Seen in the study
Doodle-faced agents: an accessory carousel (a cowboy hat wins), the agent speaking in character ("What can I wrangle up for ya?", "Yeehaw, all complete!"), visible work (sources ticking up, a table filling, statuses flipping to Complete), then one agent pulling back into a grid of dozens.

## Pitfalls
A character with no voice (just a face). A character that never does anything. Off-brand illustrations.
