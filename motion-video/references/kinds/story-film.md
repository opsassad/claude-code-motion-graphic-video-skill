# Story film

**What it is:** a short film about a person's real day or task, with the product woven into it. Emotion first; the product earns its place in the story.
**Pick it when:** the product fits everyday life (assistants, consumer apps, connectors) and the brand wants warmth, not a feature list.
**Length:** 45–90 s. 16:9 or 4:5.

## Ask the person
- Who is the person, and what's their day (or their task) like?
- Which moments in it does the product touch? Which apps or services does it connect to?
- Can we use footage or photos (theirs, licensed, or generated), or must it be illustrated?
- Voice-over, captions, or only the on-screen conversation?

## Shapes it can take
- **One day, morning to night:** the light changes warm → dusk → night; each moment is one task solved.
- **One task, start to finish:** planning a trip, moving house, launching a shop.
- **Two parallel lives:** with and without the product, side by side or intercut.

## Worlds and looks
Real footage or photos of the places, blurred and dimmed behind the UI so text reads; UI as soft bubbles and inline cards. A day in the life (styles.md).

## Moves that often fit
`streamText` for replies; a connected app's icon popping in and becoming its UI card; a thumbnail growing into full-bleed footage (zoom-through); `develop` for photos; slow, calm holds; ending by subtraction.

## Seen in the study
A family weekend in one assistant conversation: cabin → hike → dinner → bedtime → starry night, each app (bookings, trails, groceries, audiobooks) appearing inline, footage behind, ending on the night sky and one quiet line.

## Pitfalls
Rushing (this kind needs time to read). Footage that fights the text. A story where the product could be swapped for any other.
