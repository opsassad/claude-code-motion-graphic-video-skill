# Styles

A style is a whole way of filming: where the camera stands, how text appears, what colour means, how fast it moves, how scenes join. The **genre** (genres.md) says what the video is for; the **style** says how it feels. Any genre can wear several styles. Pick one per video (or one main and one accent), and say which in the concept. When the brief is open, offer two styles as the two directions.

**A style is a look and a toolset, never a story order.** The concept (originality.md) decides the story; the style only decides how it looks and moves. Every style still follows the principles in craft.md. What changes is the recipe. patterns.md is the field guide behind these recipes: what strong launch films actually do, move by move.

---

## 1. Cinematic product film
*Feels like:* a high-end launch film. Quiet, confident, physical. The product speaks for itself.
*Best for:* product and feature launches, AI and tools, anything where using it *is* the pitch.

- **Subject:** actions, not screens. The film shows someone *doing* things (typing, choosing, sending), and screens appear only as the result.
- **No headlines.** The words on screen are the words typed into the product, or the product's own output. The demo is the message.
- **Camera:** two scales, cut hard between them:
  - **Macro:** extremely close on the input, 3–6 words filling the frame, camera low and angled (the angle is the lens's, not a tilted card), sliding with the caret as text types, far edges blurred.
  - **Wide:** many results at once (a wall of pages, designs, invoices) laid flat and small. Too small to read, on purpose: it says "look how much". The camera glides along, pushes into one, and the thing builds itself.
- **Colour:** near-monochrome (black, white, greys; dark or light). One accent colour with one meaning: "this is the thing being acted on now" (the selected item, the command, the final word).
- **Continuity:** carried by meaning (the caret, the accent glow, the same object), not by geometry. Hard cuts are fine between scale changes.
- **Shape it can take (one of many; invent your own from the concept):** escalation from one to many, then a collapse to one word. Don't default to it.
- **Tempo:** slow, readable typing at real speed; a fast montage near the end (about 0.5 s per item).
- **Ending:** take things away: many → one word glowing → logo on a plain ground. No tagline or link animation, though the link can sit quietly in the post.
- **Pitfalls:** tilting whole windows mid-frame (it distorts information, and looks bolted on); small UI text that matters; using the accent colour for decoration.

## 2. Narrated explainer, connected flow
*Feels like:* a friendly, clear product tour on the brand's own site look.
*Best for:* explaining *how* something works to customers or peers; launch posts where clarity beats mood.

- **Subject:** one object's journey through the product (an invoice: made → styled → sent → opened → paid → on the dashboard).
- **Text:** a short headline per beat ("Made in seconds."), with masked word rises. The voice-over carries the detail.
- **Camera:** medium shots of real UI on the brand's background. It zooms in to read (1.4–1.6x), glides to where results appear, and pushes in on important clicks, then pulls out as the result opens.
- **Continuity:** shared-element morphs along the real flow (a window becomes the document, the document becomes the attachment). Few or no cuts.
- **Colour:** the brand palette as it is on the site.
- **Pitfalls:** every scene the same length and distance (flat rhythm), headlines that say what the picture already says.

## 3. Card showcase
*Feels like:* a confident portfolio reel; each project gets its moment.
*Best for:* portfolios, personal brands, "everything we shipped this year", case-study teasers.

- **Subject:** a series of work, each with one animated moment from its own story.
- **Layout:** the real card from the site on one side, its signature moment on the other, plenty of space.
- **Motion:** 3D fly-in (deep z, rotateY settling on a spring), a hold, then a whip out with directional motion blur as the next one enters.
- **Shape it can take:** a concrete before/after opening, the person, the work, the mark. Invent a different one when the person's story asks for it.
- **Pitfalls:** a flat carousel (slideshow), a gimmick camera through one big world.

## 4. Walkthrough / training
*Feels like:* a patient colleague showing you the screen.
*Best for:* staff and customer training, how-tos, change notices, often watched muted on phones.

- **Camera on the real screen at true size,** moving to each control. A cursor glides, presses and ripples; the UI answers 100–200 ms later.
- **Caption card per step** in the viewer's language; "remember" callouts for rules.
- **Tempo:** slower (about 2.3 words per second); every step settles before the next.
- **Pitfalls:** going too fast; text too small for phones; skipping the "why".

## 5. Kinetic type
*Feels like:* a manifesto or announcement where the words are the visuals.
*Best for:* brand statements, event announcements, short social pieces, intros, quotes.

- **Text is the image:** big, few words per frame, cut on the beat. Masked rises, weight or width shifts, tracking tightening, words swapping in place.
- **Colour:** two or three colours, high contrast; the accent marks the key word.
- **Tempo:** locked to the music (beats at 60/BPM s), with holds on the key lines.
- **Pitfalls:** too many words per frame; motion on every word, so nothing stands out.

## 6. Data story
*Feels like:* one number made unforgettable.
*Best for:* results, impact reports, "in numbers" posts.

- **One number or comparison per scene.** Counters land as the voice says them, and bars or rings fill to the value.
- **The source on screen;** real data only, or clearly labelled as illustrative.
- **Pitfalls:** chart junk, several numbers fighting, animating before the viewer knows what the number means.

## 7. Clean light product film
*Feels like:* calm, clear, confident; the big-tech product-page look in motion.
*Best for:* feature launches, monthly updates, B2B tools, anything that must read as trustworthy.

- **Ground:** white or near-white, sometimes a faint grid or a soft brand-colour glow at one edge. Soft, large shadows under white cards.
- **Rhythm:** a short headline beat, then a product beat, alternating. Headlines type with a caret or blur in word by word, with one accent word.
- **Text and UI on the same line:** small UI cards sit inside sentences; headlines slide aside to let a card out; a typed word becomes an icon.
- **Floating fragments:** UI pieces drift around a centred headline at different depths, the far ones blurred.
- **Signature moves:** a word texture with one word in focus inside brackets; a lit path through a grid of options; steps flying in tilted and assembling into a straight card; a press ripple.
- **Ending:** lockup, then the product's icons in a ring, or "Learn more at…" alone.

## 8. Atmospheric glass
*Feels like:* premium, dreamy, effortless.
*Best for:* AI products, consumer apps, anything selling ease or delight.

- **Ground:** a soft mesh gradient (blush, periwinkle, iridescent), or dreamy photographic backdrops that change per beat (sky, clouds, hills, a lake).
- **UI:** frosted-glass panels and pills floating over the world, with large soft shadows and glowing rims.
- **Transitions:** iris, pixel-mosaic dissolve, colour shape sweeps, zoom-through into a photo.
- **Signature moves:** the prompt bar as a glowing hero object; an exploded layout of UI fragments around a phone with the camera orbiting; one line of text lifting out of a card and becoming an action chip.
- **Ending:** an iris into a darker gradient with a glowing CTA, or the logo on the gradient.

## 9. A day in the life (narrative)
*Feels like:* a short film with a product inside it.
*Best for:* assistants, connectors, consumer apps; any product that fits into everyday moments.

- **One task or conversation across a day.** The light goes warm → dusk → night, so the film has an arc without a narrator.
- **Real footage of the places,** blurred and dimmed behind the UI so the text is readable; each choice in the UI becomes the next piece of footage.
- **Calm tempo:** holds long enough to read whole sentences; the streaming text is the main motion.
- **Ending:** the quietest frame (the night sky), then one line, then the logo.

## 10. Character-led
*Feels like:* playful, human, memorable.
*Best for:* brands with an illustration system or a mascot; agents, assistants, bots.

- **The character has a personality and a voice in the copy** ("Howdy, partner!"), not just a face.
- **Personalising the character is a beat** (choosing an accessory from a carousel, naming it).
- **The character appears at each state:** thinking, working, done, celebrating.
- **One → many:** one character becomes a whole team (a grid of them).

## 11. Hero shot
*Feels like:* a car ad for a screen.
*Best for:* very short pieces (8–12 s), fintech, dashboards, a single key action.

- **One screen, one action.** A low-angle, rim-lit view of the dashboard, so big it runs off two edges, on a dark or deeply coloured ground.
- **The camera tracks the cursor** across the form; the far side is soft.
- **Lift-out:** one row lifts out of a table while the rest blurs.
- **Ending:** a macro of the primary button, glowing, pressed. A small watermark is enough branding.

## 12. Sizzle
*Feels like:* energy, range, "so much is happening".
*Best for:* event openers, category intros, year-in-review, social teasers.

- **20+ shots in 10 s** (0.25–0.5 s each), each one single moment of a product state (generating, analysing, a model list expanding).
- **Joins:** whip smears, holographic light leaks, hard cuts on the beat.
- **Mixed media:** UI, macro type, material type (debossed paper), real footage of a device.
- **Ending:** push into a screen until it's only light → the title resolves.

## Mixing styles
A main style plus a borrowed move is fine: an explainer can open with a cinematic macro on the first typed word; a card showcase can end in a kinetic-type line. Say in the concept which style leads, and don't switch the lead halfway.
