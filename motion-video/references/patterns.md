# Field guide: what top launch films actually do

Distilled from a frame-by-frame study of 15 strong product films: AI assistants, workspace tools, automation, analytics, fintech, real estate, developer infrastructure. Each was 8–90 s, made by in-house brand teams and independent motion designers. Read it before concepting. It's a vocabulary to choose from, not a checklist; the principles in craft.md decide what fits.

## 1. Ten things nearly every strong film does

1. **There is a hero object, and the camera lives around it.** The prompt bar, a payment notification, an agent character, a flowing data ribbon, a single card. One object the viewer learns to watch; everything else happens to it or comes out of it.
2. **The product's states are the visual vocabulary.** Typing → thinking → result → confirmed. Each state has its own look:
   - typing: a caret
   - thinking: pulsing dots, a sparkle, shimmering skeleton lines, a thin progress bar
   - the result: it streams, scans in, flips or builds
   - confirmed: a tick, a toast, a status flipping to Done, a number landing

   Showing the states *is* showing the product.
3. **Text and UI share the same space.**
   - A small UI card sits inline in a sentence like a word ("Design [card] agents to").
   - A headline slides aside and a link card comes out from under it.
   - A typed word turns into an icon, or app icons fly in and become letters.

   The words and the product are one line, not a caption above a screenshot.
4. **Scale changes are the rhythm.** Macro (4 words fill the frame) → medium (a window) → wide (a grid of 30 results) → macro again. Any film that stays at one distance feels flat.
5. **One ↔ many.** A single card pulls back into a grid of dozens (agents, designs, templates), or a grid collapses to the one that matters. A lit path through a grid shows "from many options, this solution".
6. **Proof is shown, not claimed:**
   - money arriving as notifications
   - counts ticking up (sources searched, results found)
   - statuses flipping down a list
   - a chart redrawing after a filter
   - a cart total changing
7. **One accent colour with one job.** It marks the active word, the selected item, the AI's output. The rest stays neutral.
8. **The ground is a world, chosen on purpose.** Three families:
   - **white or near-white with a faint grid or glow:** clear, technical, trustworthy
   - **an atmospheric gradient or mesh** (blush, periwinkle, iridescent): premium, warm
   - **photographic or footage worlds** (nature, real places, a hand holding a phone): emotional

   Dark appears as punctuation (a hard-cut word beat) or as a stage for a lit hero screen.
9. **Every beat settles.** Even the 10-second film lets the final button sit still, glowing, for half a second. Motion → hold → next.
10. **Endings subtract.** Logo alone; logo + one line; a sandwich (headline above, one line below the product window); the logo with the product's icons in a ring around it; a quiet night sky. Never a pile of asks.

## 2. Kinetic type that reads as high-end
- **Word-by-word blur-in** (blur 8–12 px → 0, rise 10–20 px), with **one accent word** in the brand colour.
- **Typed with a caret,** then one word swaps to the accent colour, or a word is replaced by an icon.
- **Scattered words assemble:** words start at different sizes, heights and positions and fly into one clean line.
- **One word grows into a sentence:** the first word appears huge in the accent colour, then shrinks and turns ink as the rest of the sentence arrives beside it.
- **The sentence slides to keep the newest word centred** as each word arrives.
- **Oversized type cropped by the frame,** sliding sideways, then shrinking to a small centred title (huge → small *is* the reveal).
- **Word texture:** a word repeated 50 times in grey fills the frame; one copy is in focus inside selection brackets with a sparkle.
- **The product's metaphor acted out on the type:** for a collaboration tool, named multiplayer cursors drag, rotate and scale the headline into place.
- **Text as a window:** letters filled with moving footage or a 3D render.
- **Letter effects as exits:** letters drop out at random; the word echoes into a fading stack; letters squeezed and blurred widen into place; a chromatic smear on entry.
- **Material type:** letters debossed into paper, then lifting off one by one.
- **Type on a design grid:** thin crosshair guides and registration marks around a single word.

## 3. Transitions seen (with what they mean)
| Transition | What it is | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Zoom-through into content** | a thumbnail photo grows to full-bleed and the film continues inside it | going from "the listing" to "the place" |
| **Push into a screen** | the camera pushes into a phone screen until it's only gradient light, then blurs to the next scene | from device to idea |
| **Pull back into the world** | the UI recedes until it's the screen of a laptop in a real set | from feature to life |
| **Zoom-out reveal** | one card → a grid of many → the app home screen | "one of many / it's a platform" |
| **Logo-shaped portal** | a logo or icon grows until its shape is a window into the next scene | brand-led change of chapter |
| **Iris** | a circle opening, with a soft lens edge | from a calm scene into a CTA world |
| **Pixel-mosaic dissolve** | blocks grow from the centre | a digital, AI-ish change |
| **Graphic shape wipes** | big soft circles; triangle "play" wedges; an hourglass of draining colour; a swirl | each shape should say something (speed, time saved, flow) |
| **Colour shape sweep** | an abstract curved gradient shape sweeps across | changing the world's colour |
| **Motion-blur smear** | text or UI whips sideways with a directional blur into the next line | fast, energetic linking |
| **Shared element / hand-off** | an object from this scene becomes the anchor of the next | following one thing through the flow |
| **Lift-out** | one row or item lifts out of its table; everything else blurs away | "this one" |
| **Card flip** | a result card flips in 3D from before to after | transformation, before/after |
| **Hard cut on a scale change** | macro ↔ wide | rhythm; carried by the caret or the accent glow |

## 4. Showing the product working
- **Prompt bar as hero:** a rim-light glow (rainbow or brand gradient) blooms around it; the camera is so close the typed request fills the frame and slides with the caret; then it pulls back or glides to the send button for the press.
- **Drag and drop with weight:** the file tilts while carried and settles flat on drop; a "Drop files here" zone lights up.
- **Menus and pickers:** the cursor opens a dropdown; the chosen row gets a highlight with a shine sweeping across it.
- **Thinking:** coloured dots, a sparkle, a card that grows taller as text streams into it, skeleton lines shimmering.
- **Generated media:** it scans in top-to-bottom in streaky lines, or goes grey skeleton → blurred → sharp; or a filmstrip of results slides past, each going full-frame with a slow push.
- **Work made visible:** source counts ticking up, result rows streaming in, a table filling column by column, statuses flipping, a waveform of bars for audio.
- **Results in the real app:** a label appearing on real-looking emails; a chart redrawing; a cart subtotal changing; a status chip turning green.
- **Inline app connections:** a connected app's icon pops in centre and *becomes* its UI card.
- **Press feedback:** a concentric ripple ring; a toggle sliding on.

## 5. Angles: when tilted screens work (and when they don't)
They work when the screen is **close, big, moving, and about action**:
- a typing close-up where the card is cropped by the frame and glides with the caret
- a low-angle hero shot of a dashboard so big it runs off two edges, rim-lit on a dark ground, with the camera tracking the cursor across a form
- long lists or scrolling content (history, model list, timeline) running away in perspective, then resolving flat
- a world that is 3D and glassy throughout

They fail when a **readable, static screen is tilted mid-frame for decoration**. The rule: an angle must either be moving and close, or resolve flat before anything needs reading.

## 6. Structures seen (examples to learn from, never templates; originality.md decides yours)
- **Launch film (15–60 s):** a hook (the pain, or the money) → the hero object → 3–5 beats, each one product state or one proof → one-to-many escalation → a quiet ending.
- **Sizzle (8–12 s):** 20+ shots of 0.25–0.5 s, each a single moment of the product's state, joined by smears and light leaks.
- **A day in the life (60–90 s):** one conversation or task across a day; the light goes warm → dusk → night, so the film has an arc without a narrator; real footage blurred behind the UI.
- **Character-led (60–90 s):** a brand character with a personality and a voice in the copy ("What can I wrangle up for ya?"); personalising the character is a beat in itself.
- **Hero shot (8–10 s):** a single action on one screen, filmed like a car ad (low angle, rim light, tracking camera), ending on the primary button pressed.
- **Monthly update (15–20 s):** kinetic type-led, with the product's own icons and metaphors acting on the words.

## 7. Small things that separate pro from amateur
- A tiny brand watermark fixed top-left for the whole film (in agency work).
- Shadows that are soft and large under white cards on white; never a hard drop shadow.
- Far things are blurred, near things are sharp: depth even in 2D layouts.
- Floating fragments drift slowly (parallax), so nothing is ever perfectly frozen except the final hold.
- Personal touches: a greeting with a name ("Hi Jane"), real-sounding requests ("explain quantum physics like I'm 10"), real-looking data.
- The CTA is plain text or a small badge, never a giant button.
