# Genres

Pick the genre first; it fixes the defaults. Then make a concept that belongs to *this* subject. The genre is a frame, not a script.

## Product launch / feature explainer
**Audience:** customers, peers, the wider industry. **Goal:** "this solved a real problem, elegantly."
- **Format:** 1920x1080, or 1080x1080 for social feeds. 45 to 70 s. 60 fps.
- **Shape:** decided by the concept (originality.md), not by this genre. One shape that has worked: a pain made visible → the reveal → a few capabilities → a payoff that echoes the hook. It's also the most predictable; prefer a shape that only this product could have.
- Rebuild the real UI from screenshots at true proportions, filmed by a camera that moves in on whatever is being said.
- **Styles that fit:** cinematic product film (mood and quality, the product speaks for itself) or narrated explainer (clarity, one object's journey with connected screens). See styles.md. Either way, follow one object or one action through the product, so it reads as a story rather than a slideshow.
- Voice: warm and proud, not an advert. Feeds autoplay muted, so decide captions with the person (burned-in, an uploaded .srt, or none).

## Portfolio / personal brand
**Audience:** recruiters, hiring managers, clients, peers. **Goal:** "this person ships real things people use."
- **Format:** 1080x1080 for feeds, about 60 s, voice-over, music under.
- **Look = the person's own website or brand**, not a motion template: same font, cards and colours.
- **A shape that has worked (not a template):** a concrete before-and-after opening with a year, a role and a number ("In 2018 I answered support calls. Today I build the tools 300 agents use every day"), not a vague slogan. Real employer and client logos where allowed. The person. A tour of their work, one signature moment each, drawn from that project's own story. The whole body of work at once. End on their mark and their link.
- A strong pattern for square: the project card on one side, its animated moment on the other, 3D fly-in, whip out with motion blur. It's a pattern, not a layout to copy; a different person's work deserves its own moments.

## Training / how-to walkthrough
**Audience:** staff or customers learning a tool, often on phones. **Goal:** "I know exactly what to click and why."
- **Format:** 1920x1080 (phones play it full screen sideways), 1 to 3 min, 30 fps to keep files small.
- **Structure:** optionally the old pain, briefly → "now" → step by step on the real screen. The camera zooms to the control; a cursor glides, presses and ripples; the UI responds. A **caption card** carries each step in the viewer's language, because many people watch without sound. Add "remember" callouts for rules people get wrong.
- Build the real UI at true size as a "world" the camera films; screenshots are the source of truth.
- Voice: friendly and slower (about 2.3 words per second). Say UI labels the way the users themselves say them.

## Update / change notice
**Goal:** "this changed; here's the new way." 30 to 90 s. Show the old way, cross it out, walk the new way once, end with one rule to remember. Built the same way as training.

## Teaser / social short
**Goal:** curiosity in under 20 s. 1080x1920 or 1080x1080. One striking signature moment, a single line, the mark. No tour.

## Animated data story
**Goal:** one number or trend becomes unforgettable. Counters, bars and rings land on the voice saying the value; one comparison per scene; the source on screen. Use real data with permission, or say clearly when it's illustrative.

## Interactive briefing (not a recorded video)
For a team reading a spec: a chaptered HTML deck where elements appear on beats (`data-b="n"`), with keyboard navigation, optional auto-advance and per-chapter voice. Use the same craft, but it's driven by the reader instead of the clock.

## Choosing format
| Channel | Shape | Length | fps |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn / X feed | 1080x1080 or 1920x1080 | under 70 s | 60 |
| WhatsApp / internal | 1920x1080 | any | 30 |
| Reels / Shorts / TikTok | 1080x1920 | under 60 s | 60 |
| YouTube / website | 1920x1080 or 3840x2160 | any | 60 |

If unsure, ask one question with two concrete options.
